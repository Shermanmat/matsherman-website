The Access Game: How Outsider Founders Get in the Room and Raise Venture Capital

A memoir and playbook by Mat Sherman

Get launch updates

I spent years building in Phoenix and hearing no from YC and the coastal funds.

The gap was never talent or traction. It was access.

Context can be taught.

Most of Silicon Valley’s rules are unwritten. They get passed down through private group chats, apprenticeships, and insider institutions. This book translates them for founders who cannot text a tier-one investor today.

Chapters One and Two

From Phoenix to the room

How a Phoenix native found Silicon Valley through the internet: a first startup, Y Combinator's early influence, landing Jason Calacanis as a first investor, and the company Eric Ries helped seed. It is optional reading, but it shows what moving inward actually takes.

Chapters Three and Four

The reality check

A warning before you start. The facts about raising venture capital are sobering, and proximity matters more than most founders admit. Then the real currency of tech, social capital: who knows you, who will vouch for you, and what every introduction costs.

Chapters Five and Six

Before the raise

The fundamentals: whether to raise at all, how much, how to price it, SAFEs versus priced rounds, the one-liner and the deck. Then node stacking, a way to build a network of advocates through accelerators, events, Twitter, and time in SF.

Chapters Seven and Eight

Running the raise

What to do and how to talk once you are in the middle of a process: mapping your network, managing intros, winning the meeting, getting the wire. Includes a one-year timeline from zero to a closed round, and the mistakes first-time founders make most often.

Chapter Nine and the Conclusion

The missing node

My take on what is actually broken about breaking in, why the obvious fixes did not work, and why I built MatCap the way I did. The book ends with a request: once you are in, hold the door open for the next outsider.

About the author

Mat Sherman is the founder and general partner of MatCap, an advisory-first pre-seed venture firm. He has spent more than a decade in the startup ecosystem, and since 2020 has worked to bring the advantages of the Bay Area to founders everywhere else.

He has made thousands of founder and investor introductions and works with more than 100 funds. Before MatCap he founded PubLoft and Seedscout, and worked at Product Hunt. He hosts the Forward Thinking Founders podcast.

A Phoenix-area native and Arizona State graduate, he lives in Scottsdale, Arizona.
